# Cron Triggers: five-field croner expressions, a one-minute missed threshold, bounded catch-up and queues, and derived jitter

Cron semantics are a contract with every Definition, so they are fixed here. Expressions have five fields in the Vixie-cron dialect plus `L`, `W`, `#` and `@daily`-style aliases, parsed by `croner`, which is maintained, follows the OCPS rules for DST and supports named time zones through `chrono-tz`; seconds and years are rejected so `0 9 * * *` means what it means in crontab. `tz` defaults to UTC, and occurrences follow its wall clock: across a DST change `0 9 * * *` stays at 09:00 local, a time the change skips fires at the first instant after the gap, and a time it repeats fires once. Validation rejects an expression that does not parse or never fires and a time zone that is not an IANA name, as `invalid_value`. An occurrence is missed when the scheduler reaches it more than one minute after it was due, which covers polling and a leader failover but not downtime. Of the occurrences that fell due, the Catch-up Policy keeps those on time plus, for `latest`, the newest missed one when none is on time, or, for `all`, every one up to the 100 most recent; older ones are dropped and logged, so a minutely Trigger back from a week of downtime starts 100 Runs, not 10,000. The Overlap Policy then decides what starts: it watches the last Run the Schedule started, `skip` drops an occurrence while that Run is active, `allow` starts it anyway, and `queue` holds up to 100 occurrences on the Schedule and starts the oldest once that Run finishes, one at a time, skipping further ones when full; catch-up and overlap compose, so `catch_up: all` with the default `overlap: skip` fires only the first missed occurrence. A future `max_concurrent_runs` (BIA-44) can hold firings back the same way. `jitter` delays each occurrence by an offset in `[0, jitter)` derived by hashing the Workflow, Trigger and occurrence rather than drawn at random, so every replica computes the same due time and tests know it, while different Schedules and occurrences still spread out. A cron Run's input is `{}` and its `trigger` is `{ "type": "cron", "id": <trigger id>, "scheduled_at": <occurrence, RFC 3339 UTC> }`, the occurrence before jitter, so a Run knows which slot it serves even when it started late or from the queue.
